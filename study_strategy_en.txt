JEE Preparation Strategy

Start with NCERT

NCERT textbooks of Class 11 and 12 are essential, especially for Chemistry. Inorganic Chemistry questions are often taken directly from NCERT, so read it line by line, including the examples and tables.

Build Concepts Before Practice

First understand the concept thoroughly, then solve problems from easy to hard. Do not jump to advanced problems before the basics are clear. Popular practice sources include previous year questions (PYQs), H.C. Verma for Physics concepts and N. Avasthi for Physical Chemistry problems.

Solve Previous Year Questions

Solve JEE Main and JEE Advanced previous year questions topic-wise. PYQs show the real difficulty and the most repeated topics. Aim to complete at least the last 10 years of papers before the exam.

Take Mock Tests

Attempt full-length mock tests in exam conditions of 3 hours from a few months before the exam. Analyse every mock test: classify mistakes as conceptual errors, calculation errors or silly mistakes. Maintain an error log notebook and revise it regularly.

Time Management

Divide study time evenly among Physics, Chemistry and Maths, and give extra time to your weakest subject. In the exam, attempt your strongest section first, do not spend too long on one question, and skip a question if you are unsure because of the negative marking.

Revision

Make short notes and formula sheets for quick revision. Revise each topic at increasing intervals, for example after 1 day, 1 week and 1 month. Revise Chemistry theory in the final weeks, as it is the fastest to score.

Health and Mindset

Sleep 7 to 8 hours, exercise regularly and take short breaks. Consistency matters more than studying for very long hours occasionally. Do not compare yourself with others; track your own progress.
